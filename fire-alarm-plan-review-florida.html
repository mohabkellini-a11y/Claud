When is a fire alarm plan review required in Florida?

A fire alarm plan review is required whenever a fire alarm system is newly installed, replaced, or substantially modified in a commercial building. Florida authorities having jurisdiction (AHJs) review the plans against the Florida Building Code and NFPA 72 before issuing a permit — and incomplete submittals are one of the most common causes of permit delays.

What Triggers It

What triggers a fire alarm plan review

  • New fire alarm system installations in commercial, industrial, and multi-family buildings
  • Full system replacements, including fire alarm control panel change-outs
  • Additions or modifications — new devices, new notification zones, expanded coverage areas
  • Change of occupancy or use that alters the building's fire alarm requirements
  • Tenant improvements where the base building system is extended or reconfigured

Minor like-for-like device replacements may not trigger a full review, but the line between “minor” and “substantial” is drawn by the local AHJ — when in doubt, ask before you build.

The Review

What the AHJ checks during plan review

Plan reviewers verify that the system design complies with NFPA 72 and the Florida Building Code as adopted locally. A complete submittal typically includes device layout and spacing calculations, battery standby calculations, notification appliance voltage-drop calculations, a complete riser diagram, the sequence of operations, and device compatibility documentation. Most Florida jurisdictions also require the drawings to bear the seal of a Florida-licensed professional engineer before a permit is issued.

Avoid Delays

Why fire alarm plans get rejected

  • Missing battery standby or voltage-drop calculations
  • Incomplete or inconsistent riser diagrams
  • Detector or notification device spacing that does not meet NFPA 72
  • Vague or missing sequence of operations
  • Drawings submitted without the required Florida PE seal

Most rejections come down to completeness, not design philosophy. A PE-reviewed package that includes every calculation the reviewer expects will clear plan review in fewer cycles — and keep the construction schedule intact.

Best Practice

How to get through review faster

Confirm the AHJ's submittal checklist before you draw — requirements vary between Florida jurisdictions, and submitting to the wrong checklist guarantees a rejection. Have a fire protection engineer review the package for code compliance before submittal, include all supporting calculations the first time, and respond to review comments with clouded revisions and a written response letter.
